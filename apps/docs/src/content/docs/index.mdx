Mavna moves USDC into Stellar. It quotes a transfer across every rail that can
carry it, tells you what will actually arrive after fees, and hands back unsigned
transactions for your wallet to sign.

Circle's CCTP is the rail documented here, and the one everything on this site
has been run against: 26 source chains on testnet, 25 on mainnet, across EVM,
Solana and Starknet. Circle's standard path charges nothing to reach Stellar, so
a quote out of this SDK is the underlying rate rather than somebody's margin on
top of it.

Other rails are being built. None of them are described here, because nothing
here describes something that has not been run.

## Two ways in

**[The SDK](/quickstart/sdk/)** is for a team that already has an interface.
You configure a network, ask for a quote, and get back a route with the amount
that lands, the fees behind it, how many signatures it will take, and the
transactions to sign. Nothing in the SDK holds a key, signs, or submits.

**[The widget](/quickstart/widget/)** is for a team that wants the whole flow.
It renders the quote, the confirmation, and the wait. React is a peer dependency
and everything else is 2.69 kB minified and brotlied, against a 12 kB ceiling
that CI enforces. It is styled entirely by CSS custom properties, so it can be
made to look like your product rather than like ours.

## Before you start

None of these packages are on npm yet and the `@mavna` scope does not exist.
[What is published](/install/) says exactly what you can install today and how,
and both quickstarts assume you have read it.

## What the SDK will not do

It will not sign. It will not submit. It will not take custody of anything at any
point, which is a property you can check by reading the packages rather than a
claim you have to accept: nothing under `packages/` imports a signer.

That means the last step of every integration is yours. The SDK gives you a
payload and tells you, in words meant for a person, what signing it will do.
