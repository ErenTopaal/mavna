---
title: Conversion on Stellar
description: How a transfer arrives as another asset than the one a rail carries, through Stellar exchanges in a fixed order of preference.
---

A rail delivers what it carries: Circle delivers USDC, LayerZero delivers USDT0. When the sender wants
to end up with another asset, such as XLM, a conversion on a Stellar exchange is added to the route
as the last step of the same transfer.

## The exchanges

`@mavna/swap` puts every exchange behind one `SwapProvider` interface: `quote` prices a conversion,
and `build` turns the quote into the unsigned transaction that runs it, with the sender's minimum
after slippage as a hard floor. If the market moves past the floor, the conversion fails whole and
the account keeps what arrived.

`createSwapVenues` builds the list of exchanges for a network, in this order of preference:

1. **Soroswap**, wherever it serves. It aggregates Soroswap, Phoenix, Aquarius, SushiSwap and Comet
   pools, and Stellar's order book when the money goes to an account. Its API needs a key that must
   never reach a browser, so `SoroswapProvider` calls the sponsor's
   [Soroswap proxy](/operate/api#soroswap-proxy). A sponsor without a key has no proxy, and the list
   starts at the next exchange. Before the account signs what Soroswap built, the provider checks
   that it is a single call of the expected kind with the quoted amounts, the quoted tokens, the
   account as the one paid, and a minimum no lower than the sender's floor.
2. **SushiSwap**, on the main network, for pairs that include USDT0.
3. **Aquarius**, on both networks. It routes through Soroban pools, so it can deliver to a contract
   address.

The exchanges are asked at the same time, and the answers are read in this order: the first that
is usable and quotes wins, even when a later one would quote more. The order is a preference, not
a price race.

## How a conversion joins a route

`attachSwapLeg` in `@mavna/routing` runs after a rail quotes. It prices the conversion on what the
rail guarantees to deliver, rewrites the route's `receives` and `receivesAtLeast` in the wanted
asset, appends a `swap` step, and adds one signature, because the account signs the conversion
itself.

It turns the route into a refusal, with the reason, when:

- no exchange can quote the conversion (`no-liquidity`);
- nothing priced it within the rail's time budget (`timed-out`);
- the rail delivers another asset than the conversion sells (`asset-unsupported`);
- a new account's reserve is repaid from the arrival and the wanted asset is not XLM
  (`asset-unsupported`), because the repayment is priced in XLM;
- too little would be left to convert after the repayment (`amount-too-small`).

An exchange's own error text can carry raw node output, so it goes only into the refusal's `detail`,
never its `explanation`.

When the transfer reaches the conversion, the widget asks the exchanges for a fresh price, refuses
to continue if it is below the minimum the sender was shown, and has the account sign the transaction
the winning exchange builds.

## Converting on arrival

A LayerZero route can convert on arrival instead: the money is delivered to the
[Arrival Contract](/how-it-works/arrival-contract), which converts through Aquarius with the sender's
minimum enforced inside the contract. This adds no step and no signature. If the minimum cannot be
met, the contract sends the arriving token on as it came.
