`@mavna/adapter-cctp`, adapter id `cctp`, trust model `native-mint`. Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer
Protocol burns USDC on the source chain and Circle mints the same amount on Stellar once it attests
the burn. Nobody holds the money in between, and what arrives is Circle's own USDC.

```ts
const cctp = new CctpAdapter({ network, rpc, baseReserveStroops });
```

## Chains

The adapter moves USDC from 26 chains on each network: 24 EVM chains, Solana and Starknet. Each
chain's entry carries its Circle domain number, its `TokenMessengerV2` address and its USDC
contract. [Rails and networks](/how-it-works/networks#source-chains) lists them.

The EVM list comes from `scripts/verify-cctp-chains.mjs`, which records no address it has not
checked on the chain itself: that there is code at the `TokenMessengerV2` address, which minter
that contract names, and which token the minter calls local USDC, and that the token reports itself
as USDC with six decimals. Run it with `node scripts/verify-cctp-chains.mjs`.

## The burn

The burn's `mintRecipient` and `destinationCaller` are both Circle's Stellar forwarder contract, not
the sender's address, and the sender's address travels inside the message as hook data. Circle mints to
the forwarder, which passes the USDC on to the recipient. The hook data is validated before it is
encoded, and an address the forwarder cannot deliver to is refused.

Before the burn, the sender approves the exact amount for `TokenMessengerV2`, never an unlimited
allowance. The approval is skipped when the wallet's allowance already covers the amount.

## Fees

The fee is read from Circle's fee service on every quote, for the standard path.

## Delivery on Stellar

Circle's own forwarding service does not deliver to Stellar, so someone on Stellar has to submit
the mint once Circle attests the burn. With a sponsor that covers costs, the
[sponsor](/operate/api#post-deliver) submits it and pays its fee. With
`MAVNA_RESERVE_FUNDING=self`, the receiving account signs and pays for it. Either way, the
recipient is fixed inside the attested message, so whoever submits it cannot redirect anything.

[Delivery](/how-it-works/delivery) explains how arrival is proven and why a delivery cannot happen
twice.
