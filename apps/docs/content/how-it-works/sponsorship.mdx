---
title: Sponsorship
description: How a Stellar address with no XLM receives a transfer. The reserve is a loan, and its repayment is signed before the loan is made.
---

A Stellar account needs XLM locked as a reserve before it can exist and hold an asset, and someone
arriving from another chain has no XLM. Sponsorship covers that: the sponsor provides the reserve,
and the arriving money pays it back. One rule shapes the mechanism: **the reserve is a loan, and its
repayment is signed before the loan is made.**

## What it costs

Stellar locks a reserve per unit of ledger space: an account takes two units and each trustline one.
At the base reserve of 0.5 XLM per unit that `@mavna/stellar`'s network configuration uses by
default, a new account that holds one asset locks 1.5 XLM. The route lists it as an
`account-reserve` fee, a kind of its own, because the XLM ends up locked in the sender's own account.

`inspectDestination` in `@mavna/stellar` reads in one pass what a destination needs:

| Lane | Meaning | Reserve units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ready` | an account that can take the asset now | 0 |
| `contract-ready` | a contract that exists on this network | 0 |
| `needs-trustline` | an account that cannot hold the asset yet | 1 |
| `needs-account` | no account at all | 3, or 2 when the asset is XLM |
| `unusable` | cannot receive, with the reason: for example, no contract behind a contract address, or a trustline the issuer has not authorised or that has no room left | 0 |

## The assets it lends

The sponsor sets up a trustline only to an asset its repayment can be made in: USDC, and on the main
network USDT0. The repayment spends the asset the trustline was opened for, the one the rail
delivers, so a USDT0 arrival repays its reserve in USDT0. `GET /health` lists them as `lends`, and
the widget offers a setup only for a route that delivers one of them.

The repayment's path payment trades directly between that asset and XLM on Stellar's exchange,
through no other asset. So when the sponsor builds a repayment it asks Horizon what that direct
trade costs for the reserve's XLM, and builds only when it costs less than the most the repayment
can spend, with 1% to spare. The widget asks the same before it offers the route.

## The smallest transfer

A transfer that needs sponsoring has a floor, checked at quote time: four times the reserve,
valued at the current XLM price. The price is read from Stellar's own order book through Horizon,
as the average over the first 1,000 XLM of bids and of asks, so a single small order cannot move it.
Below the floor the route declines with the smallest amount that works, for example "Send at least 6
XLM of value, or send to an address that already has one."

## The order

1. **The repayment, first.** While the sender is present, the receiving account signs a repayment
   transaction, built by the sponsor on one of its channel accounts. Its path payment buys back
   exactly the XLM the reserve needs from the arriving asset, and spends at most twice its value at
   the current price, so the account's exposure is a number it sees before signing. The signed
   repayment is held, unsubmitted, because the money it spends has not arrived. The sponsor refuses
   to set up any account it holds no repayment for.
2. **The send.** The sender signs the source-chain transactions.
3. **The setup, as late as possible.** One transaction, sourced and paid by the sponsor, creates the
   account with a zero starting balance and adds the trustline, wrapped in sponsorship operations.
   The account co-signs it, at no cost. On the Circle rail this happens after Circle attests the
   transfer, so the sponsor puts its XLM out only for a burn Circle has confirmed; on rails that deliver by themselves it happens
   before the send, because they cannot deliver to an account that does not exist. A setup adds at
   most two trustlines.
4. **The repayment lands.** Once the money arrives, the held repayment is countersigned by the
   channel and the sponsor and submitted. Its operations hand the reserves over to the account, so
   the account holds its own reserve and owes nothing.

For Circle, the sponsor submits the repayment together with the delivery. For LayerZero and Axelar,
which deliver without the sponsor, `POST /settle` and the sponsor's background sweep submit it once
the money has arrived.

## What a failure costs

A repayment signed and never sent commits nothing. A delivery that lands while its repayment cannot
leaves the sender with everything that arrived and the sponsor still carrying the reserve; such a
sponsorship counts as a stranding.

An issuer can stop a repayment. The USDC and USDT0 issuers can both freeze a trustline
(`auth_revocable`), and USDT0's can also claw a balance back (`auth_clawback_enabled`), so every
USDT0 trustline the sponsor opens is one its issuer can claw back from. A frozen trustline, or a
balance clawed back below what the repayment needs, cannot pay it. The sponsor then submits
nothing, so the repayment stays usable, and its log says once that the trustline is not authorised
by its issuer. It settles if the issuer authorises the trustline again or more of the asset
arrives. If the repayment's one-day limit passes first, it is a stranding, and the log names the
account, the reserve and why: a trustline its issuer froze, or what the account held against what
the repayment needed. Daily caps on new sponsorships and on strandings bound what the
sponsor can lose; [Capacity and caps](/operate/capacity) describes them.

## Where it runs

`buildPreparation` and `buildSettlement` in `@mavna/stellar` build the two transactions and return
them unsigned. The key, the held repayments, the caps and the delivery live in the
[sponsor service](/operate), which you can run yourself.
