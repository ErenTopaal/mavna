# Send to a new address

A Stellar address that does not exist yet, holding no XLM, can still receive your
transfer. You do not buy XLM first, and the word "trustline" never appears as a
task. This is the part no other Stellar product does.

## Why it usually cannot be done

A Stellar account has to lock a small reserve in XLM to exist and to be able to hold
an asset, and someone arriving from another chain has no XLM to lock it with. Mavna
completes the transfer anyway: a sponsor fronts the reserve, and the reserve repays
itself out of the arriving funds.

## What you do

Paste the new `G...` address as the recipient, exactly as you would an existing one.
The quote grows one line, **Kept in your account**, showing the reserve in XLM: 1.5
XLM for a new account able to hold one asset. The review summary says it plainly:

> You need no XLM to start. The reserve is yours, refunded if you close the account.

Because it is a new account, the receiving Stellar wallet signs twice, and the page
walks you through both:

1. **Approve the repayment first**, before anything is sent. This is the transaction
   that repays whoever fronted the reserve, out of the funds about to arrive. You
   agree to the most it can cost up front.
2. **Set the account up**, after the transfer is attested. This creates the account
   and opens it to receive. It costs you nothing.

The source-chain prompts happen in between, in your USDC wallet, exactly as for any
transfer.

## What it costs you

Nothing beyond the transfer itself. The reserve is not a fee anyone keeps: it ends
up locked in the new account as its own reserve, and Stellar returns it if the
account is ever closed. A small slice of the arriving funds buys the reserve back at
the live rate, capped at an amount you agreed to when you signed.
[What a transfer costs](/guides/costs) breaks down every line, and [how sponsorship
works](/developers/concepts/sponsorship) is the mechanism.
