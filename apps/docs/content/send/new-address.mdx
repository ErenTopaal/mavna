---
title: Send to a new Stellar address
description: Send to a Stellar address that does not exist yet and holds no XLM. You do not need to buy XLM first.
---

A Stellar address that does not exist on the network yet can still receive your transfer, and you
do not need to buy XLM first. Stellar needs a small XLM reserve before an account can exist and hold
an asset. The sponsor that the app works with covers that reserve up front, and a small part of the money that arrives
pays it back.

## What you do

Paste the new address as the **Recipient**, exactly as you would any other address. The route
gains one line, **Account reserve**: 1.50 XLM for a new account that holds one asset. The review
shows the same amount as **Kept in your account**, because the XLM stays in the new account as its
reserve.

You need the Stellar wallet that holds the new address, because the account approves two steps of
its own. [Connect your wallets](/send/wallets) covers connecting it.

## The prompts

For a Circle route, the card asks for these, in this order:

1. **Approve the repayment first**, in your Stellar wallet, before anything is sent. This approves
   the transaction that pays back the reserve out of the money that arrives. The card shows the
   most it can spend before you sign.
2. **Allow Circle to move your USDC** and **Send from** your chain, in the wallet that holds your
   USDC, as for any transfer.
3. **Set the account up**, in your Stellar wallet, once Circle has attested the transfer. This
   creates the account and lets it hold USDC. It costs you nothing.

On other rails the account is set up before the send instead, so both Stellar prompts come first.

Until the account is set up, closing the page pauses the transfer, and the card says so. You can
finish it later from [Activity](/send/finish).

## Small transfers

Setting up an account is only offered when enough arrives to pay the reserve back with room to
spare. Below that, the route declines with the smallest amount that works, for example "Send at
least 6 XLM of value, or send to an address that already has one."

## When the sponsor does not cover reserves

A Mavna deployment can run with its sponsor covering no reserves. The quote then declines an
address that does not exist yet and says how much XLM to send it first. An existing account that
cannot hold the asset yet allows it itself, and the review shows the cost as
**Reserve, from your own XLM**.
